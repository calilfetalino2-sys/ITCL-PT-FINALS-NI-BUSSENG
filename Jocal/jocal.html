Vaping: The Pros, Cons and What We Know

Vaping may help some adults stop smoking, but it is not risk-free. Here is what to know about the potential benefits and concerns.

Tap or click to read the news

Health desk — As vaping becomes more common, questions remain about who may benefit and what risks come with using e-cigarettes. Health experts stress that vaping and smoking are not the same, but neither is harmless.

Potential pros

  • For adults who smoke, switching completely to nicotine vaping can reduce exposure to many harmful chemicals produced when tobacco burns.
  • Nicotine vapes can help some adults quit smoking. They may work better for some people than nicotine-replacement products such as patches or gum.
  • Vapes do not burn tobacco, so their aerosol generally contains fewer harmful chemicals than cigarette smoke.

Important cons

  • Most vapes contain nicotine, which is addictive and can make it difficult to stop using them.
  • Vape aerosol is not just harmless water vapour. It can contain substances that may irritate or harm the lungs, and the long-term effects are still being studied.
  • Vaping is not recommended for people who do not smoke, especially young people, or during pregnancy.
  • Using both cigarettes and vapes may not provide the same health benefit as switching completely away from smoking.

The takeaway: Adults who smoke may reduce harm by switching completely to vaping, but people who do not smoke should not start. Anyone looking to quit can speak with a healthcare professional or local stop-smoking service about support.

Sources: NHS guidance on using e-cigarettes to stop smoking; CDC information on e-cigarette health effects.

Cigarette Smoking: The Facts Behind the Habit

Cigarettes can cause serious disease and nicotine dependence. Here is what health experts say about the risks, secondhand smoke and quitting.

Tap or click to read the news

Health desk — Cigarette smoking remains a major preventable cause of illness. Burning tobacco releases nicotine and thousands of chemicals, including many that are toxic or cancer-causing. There are no safe tobacco products.

Are there any pros?

Smoking has no health benefits. Some people may feel temporary pleasure or relief from nicotine, but nicotine is addictive, and those short-lived effects do not outweigh the health risks. People who smoke can get help to manage cravings and quit.

What are the risks?

  • Smoking causes cancers, heart disease, stroke, and lung diseases such as emphysema and chronic bronchitis.
  • Nicotine can lead to dependence, making it difficult to stop even when a person wants to quit.
  • Secondhand smoke can harm people nearby. There is no known safe level of exposure to secondhand tobacco smoke.
  • Smoking during pregnancy can cause harm to both the pregnant person and the developing baby.

Quitting can help

Stopping smoking improves health at any age. Support from a healthcare professional, counselling, and approved quit-smoking medicines can make quitting more manageable. A local stop-smoking service can help people choose a plan.

The takeaway: Cigarettes have no health upside, but quitting brings benefits. If you smoke, ask a healthcare professional or stop-smoking service about support that suits you.

Sources: CDC information on cigarette smoking and health; World Health Organization tobacco fact sheet.
